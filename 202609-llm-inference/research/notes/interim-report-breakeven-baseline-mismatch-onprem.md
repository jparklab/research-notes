# Interim report: breakeven-baseline-mismatch-onprem

**Locus question:** Which comparison baseline (hourly cloud GPU rental, the same open model bought as an API, or a closed frontier API) makes on-premise pay back, at what utilization, and does a falling price curve defeat multi-year paybacks?
**Flavor:** dialectical

## What the corpus already said

The width sweep held two analyses with sound arithmetic and opposite verdicts: Lenovo's TCO paper [[on-premise-vs-cloud-generative-ai-total-cost-of-ownership-2026-edition-lenovo-pr]] (payback in months) and the Ask This Guy blog [[on-premise-llm-and-rag-for-the-enterprise-what-60k-really-buys-you-ask-this-guy]] (buy wins in almost every interactive case). The Daily Brief piece [[best-air-gapped-llm-stack-apache-weights-on-vllm-beat-nvidias-fee-the-daily-brie]] sides with Ask This Guy. a16z's LLMflation [[welcome-to-llmflation-llm-inference-cost-is-going-down-fast-andreessen-horowitz]] supplies the price-decline term. Three arXiv papers [[muxflow-efficient-and-safe-gpu-sharing-in]], [[conserve-fine-grained-gpu-harvesting-for-llm-online-and-offline-co-serving]], [[deepshare-assurance-driven-deep-learning-job]] supply utilization proxies. Two vendor case stories [[private-ai-platform-for-200-employee-engineering-firm]] and [[sovereign-llm-platform-west-african-tier-1-bank]] supply "payback" claims. All figures below were re-checked against note bodies; corrections to the brief are flagged.

## What the new sources say

**Lenovo LP2368 (vendor whitepaper, 24 Jul 2026).** Verified: Config B 8xH200 at $397,801.60; Azure ND96isr H200 v5 at $114.65 on-demand, $73.39 / $50.33 / $46.56 for 1/3/5-yr reserved; on-prem opex $9.80/h ("$5.45 amortized maintenance + $2.27 power and cooling + $2.08 colocation"). Break-evens 3,793 / 6,250 / 9,800 / 10,800 hours = 5.2 / 8.5 / 13.4 / 14.8 months. Maintenance 12%/yr, 5-year life, "$0 recovery". Cloud is "Hourly instance rates _only_... we explicitly exclude storage costs, data egress fees, and support plans". Three structural facts the headline hides: (1) the hours-to-break-even are continuous 24/7 hours, so 5.2 months is a 100%-utilization statement; (2) no staff line at all; (3) the token-economics section divides MLPerf peak throughput into 100%-utilized hourly cost ($18.88/h / 32,955 tok/s = $0.159 per M tokens for Llama-70B) and compares to "GPT-5.4 nano API ~$1.25/M" and DeepSeek-R1 API $2.19/M, i.e. a cheap closed model and a first-party open-model API, not a commodity host of the same weights. The paper is also internally inconsistent: abstract says "under four months", executive summary says "as little as 6 months", Case 3 mislabels the config and names Google Cloud against an AWS rate. Its one customer, Onetera, ran a 40-day pilot where "a single active GPU effortlessly absorbed the initial baseline traffic" with 7 of 8 GPUs idle; its quoted 60-70% saving is versus renting "dedicated, sovereign cloud GPU capacity ($12,000 to $18,000 per month)". Case 3 (my re-derivation) gives the only utilization-aware number: 5.3 h/day versus on-demand over 5 years; recomputed for Config B versus on-demand it is about 3.95 h/day. Versus reserved commitments, which are paid whether used or not, no such threshold is given.

**Ask This Guy (vendor-adjacent: sells hosted RAG; updated 15 Oct 2026).** Verified: 2-GPU server EUR70-90k ex-VAT (own estimate); Low/Median/High annual cost 32,500 / 64,900 / 104,300 with staff 0/30k/60k; electricity 4,478 (4-14% of total, as claimed); staff+depreciation 77-83% (median/high). Baseline is explicitly "the same model" at Scaleway: EUR0.15 in / 0.60 out, EUR0.20 blended at 8:1 RAG. Capacity: 5,863 tok/s per H100 (InferenceX, "a favourable assumption for on-premise") gives 370B tokens/yr per 2-GPU server 24/7. Break-evens 162B / 324B / 522B = 44% / 88% / 141% (all arithmetic checks). Office-hours-only yields 93B tokens, about EUR18,600 of API usage, "less than its depreciation alone". On "9 to 18 months": the blog says "they compare with a closed API. Against a GPT-5-class model, about ten times more expensive, the median threshold drops to 34 billion tokens per year", and that published studies "either count electricity only or compare with on-demand GPU rental at a US hyperscaler". That is a partial confirmation: Lenovo's 5-15 month figures are the hyperscaler-on-demand/reserved variant, not a closed-API variant; the 9-18 month range itself is not found in Lenovo and is better matched to arXiv 2509.18101 (below). The same blog also supplies the missing rental baseline: H100 EUR2.80/h OVHcloud, EUR2.87/h Scaleway, versus $6.88/h AWS and $12.29/h Azure per GPU (Vantage); 2xH100 rented 24/7 at OVH about EUR49k/yr. Caveat: the author benefits from the buy conclusion, and its 10x price ratio and staff fractions are its own estimates.

**The Daily Brief (B. Rajesh, 28 Sep 2026; secondary analysis).** Verified: gpt-oss-120b, 1B tokens/month (700M in / 300M out) = $285/month = about $3,400/yr at Together's $0.15/$0.60; 8xH200 node about $370k (reseller estimate) over 3 years = about $123k/yr before power; NVIDIA AIE $4,500 per GPU-year = $36,000/yr for 8. Caveats I add: the 30-40x is an artefact of sizing a whole node for 1B tokens/month (about 386 tok/s average), which the piece concedes; right-sized to 2 GPUs (about $31k/yr) the gap is about 9x. NVIDIA's own pricing page [[pricing-nvidia-enterprise-licensing-guide]] footnotes that a 5-year AIE subscription is "Included with NVIDIA H100, H200 NVL and A800 PCIe GPUs", so the $36k/yr applies to SXM HGX nodes or other cards, not NVL cards; the licence is also avoidable with DIY vLLM. Derived crossover (mine): $31k/yr at $0.285/M blended is about 109B tokens/yr = 9.1B tokens/month = about 3.5k tok/s sustained, hardware only. Ask This Guy's low case (EUR32.5k at EUR0.20/M) gives 13.5B/month. Two independent analyses therefore triangulate to roughly 9-14B tokens/month per 2-GPU class server just to match a cheap hosted open-weight API, before staff.

**arXiv 2509.18101, "Cost-Benefit Analysis of On-Premise LLMs" [[a-cost-benefit-analysis-of-on-premise-large]].** Abstract: "break-even periods typically within a few months for small models, 2 years for medium models and 5 years for larger models... viable primarily for organizations with extreme high-volume processing requirements (>=50M tokens/month) or strict data residency mandates." Medium models "3.8 to 34 months depending on provider comparison". The study pairs nine open models against six commercial APIs (GPT-5, Claude 4 Opus/Sonnet, Grok-4, Gemini 2.5 Pro per the fetcher; I verified the range and threshold but not the provider list line by line). Its cost model assumes "8 hours/day and 20 days/month" for electricity only and takes API prices as fixed. Its 50M-tokens/month threshold is about 200x below the 9-14B/month crossover against a commodity open-weight API, which is the clearest quantitative demonstration of the baseline mismatch: moving the baseline from a closed frontier API to the same open model moves the break-even volume by two orders of magnitude.

**Utilization sources.** Beyond Per-Token Pricing [[beyond-per-token-pricing]] (42 vLLM benchmarks): "on identical H100 hardware, effective cost spans $0.21 to $15.25 per million output tokens... underutilization penalty of 2.5-24x across low-to-moderate enterprise offered loads (1-10 rps) and up to 36.3x near idle"; "any utilization-naive estimate understates true cost by exactly 1/U". OpenCost [[opencost-11210-first-of-a-kind-kubernetes-inference-cost-tracking-opencost-open]] gives an illustrative (vendor tool, not measured) example where at 25% utilization allocation cost is $4.00/M versus a $2.00 API, competitive above about 50%. Introl [[gpu-cloud-prices-collapse-introl-blog]] (vendor blog, low authority) gives a rule of thumb "<50% utilization rent; >70% evaluate ownership" and an H100 rental fall from about $8/h to $2.85-3.50/h (Silicon Data index $3.06 Sep 2024 to $2.36 Jun 2025, secondary). The fetcher also saw, unverified, search snippets that 2026 H100 contract rates rose ($1.70 to $2.35 per hour on one-year contracts Oct 2025 to Mar 2026) amid the memory shortage; Ask This Guy independently reports RTX PRO 6000 MSRP doubling to $16,000 and TrendForce server DRAM +13-18% in Q3 2026. So rental and purchase prices are not on a simple downward line in 2026.

**Production-trace proxies (NOT on-prem LLM measurements; audited absence (g) stands).** MuxFlow: CompanyX, "more than 20,000 GPUs", heterogeneous T4/A10, DL online workloads such as recommendation and machine translation (2023, pre-LLM); "both GPU utilization and SM utilization are lower than 60% for more than 99% GPUs"; sharing raised utilization from 26% to 76%. DeepShare: Alibaba PAI "more than 75% of tasks request less than 10% of a GPU, while average utilization remains only 25-50%"; its own university cluster of 219 V100/A100 GPUs averaged 19.8% (a training-heavy research cluster). Conserve: one H100 serving Llama-3.1 8B, "under a moderate load of 2000 tokens/s (average load...), GPU utilization drops sharply, and over 70% of resources are left idle" versus a good operating point near 5000 tok/s; it cites diurnal ChatGPT traffic. Only Conserve is LLM-specific, and it is a single-GPU simulation of a diurnal trace. All are proxies for the order of magnitude (20-50% typical, 76% reachable only with co-scheduling of batch work), not a measured on-prem LLM cluster.

**Price-decline sources.** a16z (Nov 2024): "For an LLM of equivalent performance, the cost is decreasing by 10x every year", measured as the cheapest model clearing MMLU 42 (GPT-3 $60/M in 2021 to Llama 3.2 3B at $0.06/M) and MMLU 83 (62x since March 2023); the authors concede MMLU contamination, that "the rate may slow", and list drivers including "Smaller models", quantization, software, and open-source price competition. Epoch [[llm-inference-prices-have-fallen-rapidly-but-unequally-across-tasks-epoch-ai]]: "ranging from 9x to 900x per year" depending on milestone, 40x/yr for GPT-4-level on GPQA, and "the fastest price drops... have occurred in the past year, so it's less clear that those will persist." Tiered Super-Moore [[tiered-super-moores-law-price-evolution-production]]: about 600x decline 2020-2026; price half-life 1.10 years (economy) and 1.55 years (mid-tier); flagship fit R2=0.031 because of a 31.5x reasoning premium; cost decomposition attributes about 103.7% to total-factor productivity and "GPU hardware contributing only -0.9%", i.e. software/model/competition, not silicon. Price of Progress [[the-price-of-progress]] (MIT FutureTech, via fetcher): fixed-benchmark prices falling 5-10x/yr while frontier-running prices rise 3-18x/yr.

**Deployments.** Particula's 200-person German firm: 4xL40S server, EUR48k total, L40S at "roughly EUR 7,500 per card versus EUR 28,000+ for an H100", about 2 kW, about EUR200/month electricity, 30-50 concurrent peak, Qwen3-32B/8B on vLLM with Ollama managing models. Its "<4 month" payback is EUR48k / (EUR14,000 per month cloud spend - EUR400 per month) = 3.5 months, where the baseline is the firm's existing SaaS: ChatGPT Enterprise seats, GitHub Copilot, transcription services. This is a fourth baseline (per-seat SaaS, with about 80 employees on shadow ChatGPT accounts), assumes all EUR14k is displaced by Qwen3-32B-class models, and counts neither staff time nor the vendor's own delivery fee. The West African bank (MindMap vendor story): 32 H100 for Llama 3.1 70B in TP groups of four plus an L40S fleet for 8B/7B models, 40+ use cases, "62% lower than the equivalent cost on the bank's previous mix of public cloud LLM endpoints". Baseline = a heterogeneous pre-existing endpoint mix (probably including closed models), no capex, utilization, or staffing disclosed. Unverifiable; use as an existence proof of the shared-platform pattern, not as a cost datum.

## Evidence synthesis

The disagreement is not arithmetic; it is the choice of denominator. Four baselines appear: (A) hyperscaler on-demand/reserved GPU-hours (Lenovo: payback 5-15 months, at 100% duty); (B) the same open weights via a commodity API (Ask This Guy, Daily Brief: break-even needs about 44-88% of 24/7 capacity or 9-14B tokens/month per 2-GPU server, hardware-only to staff-loaded); (C) a closed frontier API (arXiv 2509.18101 and the Onetera/Lenovo token tables: break-even at tens of millions of tokens per month, months to 5 years); (D) existing SaaS seats (Particula 3.5 months). The ordering A,C,D << B in apparent payback is explained by price ratios, not by engineering: hyperscaler H200 list is about $14/GPU-hour versus about EUR2.8/H100-hour at European neoclouds (about 2.4-4x on the Ask This Guy numbers), closed APIs are about 10x the price of open-weight hosting per Ask This Guy's estimate, and commodity open-weight APIs ($0.15/$0.60 per M for gpt-oss-120b at both Scaleway and Together) are priced near marginal hardware cost.

Utilization is the term that moves everything and the one nobody measured. Anything above 100% of a vendor's MLPerf/InferenceX peak is impossible and even 50% requires a continuous background load; interactive office-hours chat caps duty at roughly 25-30% (Ask This Guy: 93B of 370B tokens). The proxies (20-50% typical, 26% to 76% only with co-scheduling) are consistent with this. Applying 1/U to Lenovo's own $0.159/M gives $0.32 at 50%, $0.64 at 25% and $1.59 at 10%, against a commodity open API at $0.20-0.29 blended: Lenovo's own numbers break even against baseline B only above roughly 55-80% sustained utilization (my derivation, hardware only, MLPerf-peak throughput, different models), matching Ask This Guy's 44-88%. That the two "opposed" analyses agree when put on the same baseline is the main finding.

The price-decline question is smaller than it sounds for this decision. The 10x/yr is "constant capability", not "constant model": it is substantially delivered by newer, smaller, better-trained models (a16z lists it among drivers; Tiered Super-Moore attributes about all of it to non-hardware productivity). A self-hoster can adopt the same new open weights and engines (vLLM, quantization) on the hardware already owned, so the capability-adjusted decline partly accrues to on-prem too. What does not accrue to on-prem is competitive margin compression at API hosts for a fixed model, and hardware obsolescence (InferenceX: B200 about 5x an H100 per token on gpt-oss-120b). Evidence on the fixed-model API price path is thin: identical $0.15/$0.60 at two providers suggests a floor, but no series was found. Declines are also uneven (9x-900x), possibly not persistent, and flagship prices are flat or rising. Conclusion: falling prices defeat 3-5 year paybacks computed against a fixed API price (2509.18101 assumes fixed prices), and they make Lenovo's 5-year-reserved line (46.56 $/h locked) irrelevant, but they do not mechanically produce a 10x/yr erosion of the same-model break-even.

### Position A (on-prem pays back quickly): Lenovo/Onetera
Best case: the 8xH200 node at $398k vs $114.65/h on-demand, 24/7 steady workload, breaks even at 3,793 hours; against 5-year reserved still 14.8 months; 5-year $0 residual is conservative; a shared platform (the bank) amortizes staff across 40 use cases; closed APIs are 8-17x more costly per output token for tasks a 70B open model handles. For batch pipelines (OCR $2.00 to $0.20 per 1,000 pages) it is plausible. Weakness: 100% duty, no staff, hyperscaler list prices, vendor authorship, internal inconsistency.

### Position B (buy wins at equal model): Ask This Guy / Daily Brief
Best case: comparing the same weights removes the quality confound; commodity open APIs are cheap enough that a node needs 44-88% of 24/7 capacity; staff plus depreciation are 77-83% of cost; hardware loses ground to Blackwell generation turnover. Weakness: both authors sell or recommend hosted alternatives; ATG's staff and 10x ratio are estimates; the model assumes a 2-GPU gpt-oss-120b workload, not 40-use-case platforms or models with no commodity host; the comparison excludes data-residency and air-gap value and ignores egress/rate-limit/latency terms.

## Committed position

For an internal deployment, the correct baseline is the same open-weight model (or the best open model that passes your own offline eval) bought as a jurisdiction-compliant hosted API or as dedicated GPUs from a non-hyperscaler cloud, and against that baseline on-premise pays back only for sustained, mostly batch or multi-tenant platform load. The hyperscaler-hourly baseline answers a different question (own vs rent hardware, at 24/7 duty and list price), and the closed frontier baseline measures a quality premium, not an infrastructure saving, which on-prem cannot capture anyway because closed weights cannot be installed (Gemini on GDC aside). When the constraint is legal (air-gap, residency, contractual), there is no baseline and cost is the price of the constraint; the Daily Brief's "you air-gap because you are not allowed to call an API, never to save money" is the right framing. The load-bearing reason is that the same-model API is the only comparison where capability is held constant and where the seller's price is already near marginal hardware cost, and on it Lenovo's and Ask This Guy's numbers converge at roughly 55-80% sustained utilization of peak throughput (about 9-14B tokens per month per 2-GPU server), a level no published trace shows interactive enterprise load reaching. The price-decline curve is a secondary threat: it weakens multi-year paybacks computed against fixed API prices, but because the decline is mainly software and model improvement, owners can capture part of it; the durable risk is hardware generation turnover and the 1/U penalty.

- **Position:** Use same-open-model-as-API (and neocloud dedicated-GPU rental) as the decision baseline; on-prem beats it only above about 50-60% sustained utilization of peak throughput or about 9-14B tokens/month per 2-GPU server (hardware only, more with staff), otherwise it is justified by constraint, not cost.
- **Confidence:** medium (about 70%). Arithmetic converges across two independent sources and Lenovo's own figures, but all utilization inputs are assumptions, the API price points are two providers on one model, and both Position-B sources are commercially interested.
- **Boundary conditions:** Holds for interactive internal assistant/RAG workloads on 100-120B-class open models in 2026 at 2-8 GPU scale with EU/US API prices. It weakens for (i) shared multi-use-case platforms where staff and GPUs amortize (bank pattern, unverified), (ii) steady batch pipelines that fill nights and weekends (OCR, extraction, indexing), (iii) models with no commodity host, (iv) small L40S-class deployments displacing per-seat SaaS (Particula's EUR48k vs EUR14k/month, where baseline D is legitimately the right one). It is moot where air-gap is mandatory.
- **What would change this position:** a published measured utilization for a named on-prem LLM cluster above about 60% on mixed interactive load; a resale/residual-value series showing H100/H200 retaining more than about 40% of cost after 3 years (Lenovo assumes 0%, so this would help on-prem); open-weight API prices for a fixed model rising or being withdrawn (rate limits, EU hosts closing); or a staff-inclusive TCO from an organization running >=32 GPUs showing sub-$0.10/M blended at realized load.
- **Evidence weight:** Two vendor TCO documents (opposed, both interested), one reseller-style secondary analysis, one academic cost model (assumes fixed prices, closed baselines), one methodology paper that directly measures the utilization penalty (strongest on utilization), three price-trend studies (agree on direction, disagree 9-900x on rate), three DL/LLM utilization proxies (not on-prem LLM measurements), two unverifiable vendor customer stories. Nothing audited and independent supports either extreme.

## Open questions

- No resale or residual-value series for H100/H200 found; Lenovo's $0 recovery vs real secondary-market value is unresolved.
- No fixed-model (gpt-oss-120b) API price time series, so the same-model price-decline rate is unmeasured.
- 2026 H100 rental price direction (rising per unfetched snippets vs Introl's 2025 collapse) is unresolved; Introl is a low-authority blog and Silicon Data primary was not reached.
- Staff cost for multi-tenant platforms (bank pattern) is undisclosed everywhere; unfetched leads enterprisedna.co and findstack.com were not verified.
- arXiv 2607.13080 (coding agents, cached-API inversion: 99.3% cache hit cuts API cost 88.6%) was in the vault but I did not read it fully; prompt caching is a baseline-side term worth a follow-up.
