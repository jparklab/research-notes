# Interim report: oss-boundary-sharpening

**Locus question:** Where exactly does the open-source-sufficient / must-build-custom line fall across the stack, what predicts which side a component lands on, and is the line moving as the control-plane projects mature?
**Flavor:** dialectical

## What the corpus already said

The width sweep's `oss-control-plane-sufficiency` contradiction cluster already laid out both sides with first-person operator evidence on each: Side A (must build — Moonshot/Mooncake, AIBrix's Related Works, Netflix's metrics proxy, Thomson Reuters' Rust control plane, Uber/Atlassian gateways, the OTel GenAI spec's "Development" status, LiteLLM's Enterprise gate, NeMo Guardrails' own stand-alone disclaimer, vLLM's KEDA scale-to-zero caveat, DeepSeek's bespoke architecture) against Side B (adopt, don't build — Uber's Neuropod→Triton migration, Ollama's GGML→llama.cpp reversal, Tesla's StatefulSet→KServe+llm-d migration, the 6.1B-request LRU/FIFO trace, LiteLLM-in-front-of-vLLM as the standard metering pattern, KServe's "built on llm-d" framing, Cloudflare still running vLLM despite building Infire). The graph's own `scope_overlap` note already proposed the resolution I independently converged on: engine and orchestration have commoditized, gateway/policy/metering/eval have not — and flagged the control plane as the genuinely live fight because llm-d/AIBrix/Dynamo are only ~18 months old. The `engine-monoculture` and `gateway-governance-neutrality` clusters supplied two further load-bearing primary artifacts I rely on below: the GAIE/EPP governance move into llm-d's own repo (resolved May 2026), and NVIDIA Dynamo independently forking its own EPP rather than depending on the shared implementation. Consensus claims `oss-layer-originates-as-released-internal-systems` (today's OSS control plane is last cycle's donated in-house code) and `gateway-is-where-internal-teams-build` (five independent first-person gateway builds) are both load-bearing ground truth I build on rather than re-prove.

## What the new sources say

I did not need to fetch heavily outside the width corpus: an earlier research pass left a large, directly relevant sub-corpus under `llmd-kv-cache-routing-c665d5` (177 notes) and `llmd-router-nonk8s-backends-54b774` (225 notes) that the tag-filtered `note list` undercounts but that `search` surfaces. Reading the primary documents in full, rather than trusting summaries, is what let me date each side's claims precisely. I then spent part of my fetch budget confirming four live-status questions the dating exercise raised, rather than accumulating more width.

**The Moonshot/Mooncake quote, in full context** ([[mooncake-a-kvcache-centric-disaggregated-2]], arXiv:2407.00079, July 2024): "Similar reuse logic is already implemented in vLLM, but the open-source version of vLLM only supports local KVCache caching." This sentence is Moonshot's own stated justification for building Messenger (RDMA transfer), Conductor (global scheduler) and chunked pipeline parallelism rather than adopting something that already existed. In July 2024 nothing that already existed could have closed this gap — LMCache's tech report is Oct 2025 (arXiv:2510.09665), NVIDIA announced Dynamo in March 2025, and Red Hat/CoreWeave/Google/IBM/NVIDIA launched llm-d in May 2025.

**AIBrix's capability-gap claim, dated precisely** ([[aibrix-towards-scalable-cost-effective-large-2]], arXiv:2504.03648, submitted Feb 22 2025; [[introducing-aibrix-a-scalable-cost-effective-control-plane-for-vllm-vllm-blog]], Feb 21 2025): the Related Works section states Knative/Istio are "not designed for GPU-based inference workloads" (token-based rate limiting incompatible with circuit-breaker logic, QPS autoscaling blind to KV-cache pressure) and KServe/RayServe "lack deep integration with LLM inference engines... do not provide specialized optimizations for... KV cache coordination." This was accurate as of Feb 2025, before KServe had an LLM-specific CRD.

**KServe's own documentation closes exactly that gap, dated** ([[understanding-llminferenceservice-kserve]]; [[announcing-kserve-v017-production-ready-llm-serving-with-llminferenceservice-kse]], release dated March 13, 2026): KServe's LLMInferenceService CRD is "Built on the foundation of llm-d," ships an auto-created EPP running `prefix-cache-scorer` and `load-aware-scorer` plugins, PD disaggregation via NixlConnector/RDMA, TP/DP/EP parallelism explicitly for MoE models (Mixtral, DeepSeek-R1), KV-cache tracking via ZMQ `BlockStored`/`BlockRemoved` events, and a cost-aware Workload Variant Autoscaler. KServe itself joined CNCF as Incubating in Nov 2025; llm-d joined CNCF Sandbox March 12, 2026. The exact capability list AIBrix's Related Works said was missing in Feb 2025 now ships inside the neutral-CNCF-governed project 13 months later.

**LMCache's own paper confirms it closed Moonshot's specific gap for everyone, not just Moonshot** ([[lmcache-an-efficient-kv-cache-layer-for-enterprise-scale-llm-inference-2]], arXiv:2510.09665v2, Dec 2025): states plainly that LMCache is "used in — and is the actual KV-caching implementation inside — vLLM Production Stack, NVIDIA Dynamo, llm-d (Red Hat), and KServe." Over roughly a year its backend support expanded from 3 to 11 storage targets including Mooncake Store itself. And Mooncake's own current GitHub README ([[github-kvcache-aimooncake-mooncake-is-the-serving-platform-for-kimi-a-leading-ll]], v0.3.13.post1, 6.6k stars) now documents integrations with SGLang HiCache, vLLM disaggregated prefill, and LMCache — the system built in 2024 to work around the gap is now one of the interoperating pieces of the ecosystem that closed it.

A direct check of NVIDIA Dynamo's own documentation complicates the "LMCache closed it for everyone" framing slightly, in an informative way: [[kvbmmd]] (Dynamo KVBM docs, v1.0.1) describes KVBM (KV Block Manager) as a *separate, native* NVIDIA-built three-layer memory system (LLM-runtime connectors → KVBM logic → NIXL transport) spanning GPU HBM/host DRAM/RDMA/SSD/object storage, with its own block lifecycle and eviction policy — not the same artifact as LMCache. The KVBM doc's own "Next Steps" lists LMCache integration ([[lm-cachemd]]) and FlexKV integration as separate, parallel offload backends a Dynamo deployment can choose between, not a dependency chain. So the KV-cache gap closed on three independent, differently-built tracks (LMCache from academia/Tensormesh, Dynamo's native KVBM from NVIDIA, llm-d's MooncakeStoreConnector/LMCache integration from the Red Hat coalition) rather than one project winning and the others merely adopting it — which is itself a stronger signal of a genuine, generic capability gap than if a single project had become everyone's dependency.

**Not every Side-A gap has closed, and the ones that haven't are different in kind from the ones that have.** vLLM Production Stack's own current docs ([[autoscaling-with-keda-production-stack]]) still treat scale-to-zero as needing a second bolted-on "keepalive" Prometheus trigger — unresolved, current. The OpenTelemetry GenAI metrics spec ([[semantic-conventions-genaidocsgen-aigen-ai-metricsmd-at-main-open-telemetryseman]], Status "Development" as of Oct 2026) defines only client/server duration, TTFT, and token-timing — explicitly no KV-cache utilization, queue depth, or batch-size metrics. A direct check of vLLM's own issue tracker confirms this is live, contested engineering, not just unfinished spec text: [[rfc-adopt-otel-genai-semantic-conventions-for-metrics-issue-51186-vllm-projectvl]] ("[RFC]: Adopt otel genai semantic conventions for metrics," opened Aug 5 2026) is still open and unmerged as of its last comment on Sep 14 2026, where vLLM maintainer markmc raised an unresolved cardinality objection to the required `gen_ai.operation.name` label — "Given the cost of this label, what user need do we think gen_ai.operation.name actually solves for? I struggle to imagine it being sufficiently critical to justify it being a required attribute" — and redirected the energy toward OTLP export support (issue #30252) instead. Netflix-style custom metrics-translation work is therefore not a temporary gap waiting on a merge; vLLM's own maintainers are actively resisting the specific standardization mechanism that would close it, on cardinality-cost grounds that have nothing to do with maturity.

**The llm-d non-Kubernetes gap — the one capability-gap case I flagged as unresolved — turns out to have already closed, which strengthens the pattern rather than complicating it.** [[v08-release-goals-mid-june-issue-1520-llm-dllm-d]] (opened May 16 2026, closed June 23 2026 when v0.8.0 shipped) shows "Non-Kubernetes deployment mode" — "Enables llm-d in Slurm/research labs without Kubernetes (file-based discovery)" — merged via a ten-PR chain in llm-d-router plus a companion "no-kubernetes-deployment" guide (llm-d/llm-d PR #1618). So the control-plane layer closed on two independent fronts inside roughly a year: the capability AIBrix said was missing in Feb 2025 (LLM-specific scheduling on top of Kubernetes) shipped inside KServe by March 2026, and llm-d's own remaining capability limitation (Kubernetes-only) shipped four months later, in June 2026. I have found no first-person operator account of running llm-d or Dynamo outside Kubernetes yet, so "ships the feature" and "an operator relies on it in production" remain two different claims — but the capability itself is no longer missing.

**The gateway layer's "neutral standard" is itself fragmenting, dated.** [[proposal-to-move-epp-and-bbr-to-the-llm-d-repo-issue-2430-kubernetes-sigsgateway]] (opened Feb 26 2026, closed/completed May 13 2026) documents Google's own proposal to move the reference EPP and Body-Based Router out of neutral `kubernetes-sigs` into llm-d's repo. Microsoft/Istio maintainer keithmattix objected on the record: "What LLM-D doesn't provide that GAIE did is neutral governance... other inference frameworks that want to create an EPP would be faced with starting from scratch or importing a competitor's (i.e. llm-d's) EPP" — and named NVIDIA Dynamo and Ray/KubeRay as frameworks with a stake. A third commenter confirmed NVIDIA Dynamo is independently vendoring its own EPP fork rather than depending on shared code. [[envoy-ai-gateway-reaches-v10-establishing-the-open-source-standard-for-enterpris]] (PRNewswire, June 23 2026, Tetrate/Bloomberg/Nutanix as named maintainers, no single-company control) reached v1.0 GA with Bloomberg and LY Corporation as production adopters doing exactly what Uber/Atlassian built custom gateways for in 2024-2025 — but the same release explicitly lists dollar-based (not token-based) spend governance and fine-grained MCP authorization as still-roadmap, not shipped.

**Direct evidence the boundary is moving toward OSS, recently, mid-flight.** Hetzner's own engineering retrospective ([[what-we-learned-from-our-inference-experiment]], Aug 17 2026) states: "routing for inference requests was greatly improved by introducing llm-d and making changes to some routing components we had developed in-house" — an organization adopting llm-d alongside, not instead of, some retained in-house code, inside the run's recency window. [[production-grade-llm-inference-at-scale-with-kserve-llm-d-and-vllm-llm-d]] documents Tesla's naive self-managed vLLM-in-StatefulSet deployment hitting storage drag, node-affinity lock-in, and cache-blind round-robin, then migrating to KServe+llm-d+Envoy AI Gateway and measuring a 3x output-tokens/s gain and 2x TTFT reduction from prefix-cache-aware routing — Tesla contributed fixes upstream to KServe in the process (kserve#4901, #4900, #4898, #4899, #4970, #4886).

**Scale gates adoption of the heavy control-plane projects.** [[how-to-build-an-inference-platform]] (Intility, Jun 2026) explicitly chose the lighter Gateway API Inference Extension's reference EndpointPicker plus Envoy AI Gateway over Dynamo or llm-d, judging the latter "reserved for larger-scale disaggregated prefill/decode, which Intility has not yet implemented" — zero custom control-plane code, all GitOps wiring. This is the cleanest "OSS-sufficient, full stop" case in the corpus, and it is exactly the case with the least pre-existing infrastructure and the least cross-jurisdiction policy weight.

**The eval layer separates tooling from content cleanly.** [[github-promptfoopromptfoo-test-your-prompts-agents-and-rags-red-teamingpentestin]] (MIT, now organizationally under OpenAI per its own README, 25.8k stars), [[github-langfuselangfuse-open-source-agent-evals-observability-trace-evaluate-and]] (MIT core, acquired by ClickHouse Jan 2026, `ee/` gated), and [[github-arize-aiphoenix-ai-observability-evaluation-github]] (Elastic License 2.0, Arize's own product) supply fully adequate harness mechanics — CI-gating, LLM-as-judge scoring, dataset/experiment versioning. What none of them can supply is the content: [[from-production-traffic-to-post-training-building-a-self-hosted-llm]] (T-Tech) built its own production-error taxonomy (instruction-following/formatting failures at 37.9% of failures, tool-calling at ~12% of traffic) and its own per-axis GRPO training loop scored against in-house stratified benchmarks — not because Promptfoo or Langfuse are technically incapable of running such evals, but because the rubric itself is T-Tech's own traffic and product.

**Guardrails: the "stand-alone" caveat was a design philosophy, not a maturity gap, and current practice already matches it.** NeMo Guardrails' founding paper ([[nemo-guardrails-a-toolkit-for-controllable-and-safe]], arXiv:2310.10501, Oct 2023) states guardrails "should not be used as a stand-alone solution... programmable rails complement embedded rails." [[ai-guardrails-implementing-safety-for-production-llm-apps]] (June 2026) shows the field already complied via composition, not bespoke engineering: Presidio (PII) + Prompt Guard 2 (Apache 2.0, Meta) + NeMo Guardrails (dialog policy) + Llama Guard 4 (Apache 2.0, released Apr 30 2025) + a schema validator, all OSS, stacked.

**Weight distribution is solved OSS tooling, with one policy-driven exception.** [[reducing-cold-start-latency-for-llm-inference-with-nvidia-runai-model-streamer-n]] (NVIDIA, Sep 16 2025) documents Run:ai Model Streamer (OSS, already a loader option inside vLLM/TGI) roughly halving engine-ready time versus the default Safetensors loader, with native S3 support the default loader lacks entirely. [[how-to-build-an-inference-platform]] (Intility) uses Longhorn (OSS) backing a single shared `ReadWriteMany` volume so only one pod downloads weights. [[llm-model-serving-faster-inference-on-snowflake]] (Apr 17 2026) is the one exception: Snowflake built custom egress-proxy/network routing inside its airgapped Snowpark Container Services perimeter to hit 700GB-in-83s — solving its own network-perimeter constraint, not a generic weight-loading gap.

**LiteLLM's tag-based-budget gate shows no sign of moving to OSS, and the absence itself is a finding.** A direct check of LiteLLM's own GitHub found [[feature-support-persistent-tags-on-user-entity-with-automatic-propagation-to-spe]] (issue #25069, opened Apr 3 2026), which explicitly accepts the Enterprise gate as a given ("Enterprise feature is acceptable") and only requests an adjacent OSS field — it was auto-closed "not planned" by a stale-bot on Jul 10 2026 with zero maintainer engagement. This is not symmetric with every enterprise feature, though: [[mcp-gateway-move-cost-tracking-and-permission-management-to-oss-by-jugaldb-pull]] (PR #12988) shows BerriAI does sometimes move specific enterprise features into OSS — in that case MCP Gateway cost tracking and vector-store permission management, not tag-based budgets. So the monetization boundary is real and specific (tag-based budgets, named Side-A evidence, stays gated with no roadmap pressure) rather than a blanket policy — which matters because it means the boundary is a series of individual, revisable commercial choices, not a fixed architectural one.

## Evidence synthesis

### Position A: every serious operator still writes substantial code, and the gaps are specific, documented and recent

The strongest version of this position does not claim OSS is bad — it claims specific, nameable capabilities were missing at specific, recent dates, and names them exactly: AIBrix's Related Works table, Moonshot's "only supports local KVCache caching" sentence, Netflix's exact "9 of 40+ metrics" count, the OTel GenAI spec's literal "Development" status marker (and vLLM's own maintainers actively resisting the fix, on cardinality grounds, as of Sep 2026), LiteLLM's inline `# ENTERPRISE` code comment gating tag-based spend tracking (confirmed, as of this writing, to have no roadmap pressure behind it), vLLM's own KEDA tutorial needing a bolted keepalive trigger. Every one of these is a primary-source, quotable, falsifiable claim — this is unusually strong evidence for a "gaps exist" position because it isn't inference from absence, it's operators and projects naming their own limitations in their own documentation. Several of these gaps (KEDA scale-to-zero, OTel engine metrics, LiteLLM tag budgets) are confirmed still open as of the most current evidence available (Oct 2026), so the position is not merely historical.

### Position B: the adopt-don't-build direction is at least as well evidenced, and migrations away from custom code are costly, deliberate actions

The strongest version of this position rests on the same kind of evidence — primary, first-person, dated — but notes that migrations AWAY from bespoke code are a stronger signal than migrations toward it, because reversing a build decision costs real engineering time and admits a prior mistake, which companies don't do lightly. Uber deprecated its own Neuropod engine for Triton. Ollama's own commit log shows it removed its vendored GGML/CGO backend in April 2026 specifically "to more rapidly pick up new capabilities and fixes from llama.cpp as they come out" — development velocity, not capability, was the reason, and it is a reversal of an earlier custom-build decision within the recency window. Tesla moved off a naive self-managed vLLM deployment onto KServe+llm-d and measured concrete wins (3x throughput, 2x TTFT) from doing so, then upstreamed fixes rather than keeping them private. Hetzner explicitly adopted llm-d mid-2026 to improve routing it had previously built in-house. Intility never built custom control-plane code at all. llm-d itself closed its own remaining capability gap (non-Kubernetes deployment) within a single release cycle once a contributor was assigned to it. None of this is vendor marketing — it is operators' own engineering-blog accounts and projects' own issue trackers, which is about as costly and falsifiable a form of evidence as this topic can produce.

## Committed position

The capability-gap explanation and the "amount of pre-existing infrastructure" explanation are not actually rivals once you look at *which* layer each one explains — and the evidence also surfaces a fourth factor the locus brief doesn't name, which turns out to be more decisive than any of the three it does. **Capability gap predicts the speed and direction of the boundary's movement, and it moves fast: all three of this corpus's cleanest dated gaps (Moonshot's distributed-KV-cache complaint, AIBrix's "KServe lacks LLM-specific capabilities" complaint, and llm-d's own Kubernetes-only limitation) closed within 4-18 months of being stated, and the tell that a gap is a true, generic capability gap rather than an idiosyncratic need is that multiple independently-funded, differently-governed efforts converge on the same fix on different schedules — LMCache (academia/Tensormesh), Dynamo's native KVBM (NVIDIA) and llm-d's MooncakeStoreConnector integration (Red Hat coalition) all shipped distributed/cross-engine KV cache within the same ~12-month window via three structurally different implementations, not one project becoming everyone's dependency; KServe absorbed llm-d's scheduling capabilities rather than reinventing them; llm-d itself shipped non-Kubernetes support the moment a named contributor was assigned to the issue.** But capability gap does *not* explain where today's residual custom code sits, and here the pre-existing-infrastructure hypothesis beats the pure-capability story on every example that holds up to scrutiny: Netflix's custom metrics proxy exists because Netflix wraps vLLM in Triton for consistency with its company-wide Model Scoring Service, not because vLLM's own 40+ native metrics don't exist — and vLLM's own maintainers are actively declining (on cardinality-cost grounds, not laziness) to standardize that surface via OTel, so this gap is not just unclosed but being deliberately held open by the engine project itself; Atlassian's TensorRT-LLM customization and Snowflake's egress-proxy both trade a small latency/throughput win for integration with a perimeter or compilation pipeline that predates the LLM workload; Thomson Reuters' 11-cluster/4-geography Rust control plane is explicitly a data-residency-law and blast-radius architecture, not a KV-cache or scheduling architecture (its 2026 paper never mentions llm-d, KServe, Istio, or KV-cache tooling even once) — the custom build is organization-specific by construction, and no open-source project, however mature, supplies "where this org is legally allowed to route this specific request." The policy-locus hypothesis wins outright at the eval and guardrails layers specifically: T-Tech's custom benchmark rubric and GRPO training loop are not a Promptfoo-shaped gap, they're T-Tech's own definition of "good enough to ship," which is definitionally not something a generic tool can contain; LiteLLM's tag-based budget gate is the same shape of problem wearing a different cause — it's a rule about who may spend what, and even though LiteLLM's own OSS code already logs everything needed to enforce that rule, the enforcement is withheld not because it's hard but because LiteLLM-the-company chose to monetize it specifically (confirmed: a direct feature request accepting the gate as "acceptable" and asking only for an adjacent field was closed "not planned" with zero engagement), even though the same company does sometimes move other enterprise features into OSS (MCP Gateway cost tracking, per PR #12988) — which is a fourth, previously unnamed predictor: **vendor-monetization boundaries inside open-core projects, and governance-capture fights over whose implementation of a now-shared capability you depend on (the EPP/BBR move into llm-d's repo, Dynamo's independent EPP fork, Promptfoo's absorption into OpenAI, Langfuse's absorption into ClickHouse) are a separate axis from "is this capability open-source" — a component can be fully OSS-capable and still force a build-or-pay-or-depend-on-a-competitor decision, for reasons that have nothing to do with engineering maturity and will not resolve with more time, because they are decisions by the OSS supply side, not gaps the adopting organization is waiting on.** The honest layer-by-layer verdict: engine (closed/settled), control plane (closed, both the Kubernetes-native capability gap and llm-d's own non-Kubernetes limitation, as of June 2026), KV-cache distribution (closed, cleanly, via three independent implementations, 2024→2025-26), weight distribution (closed via Run:ai Model Streamer + shared-volume storage, except under a pre-existing network perimeter), guardrails (closed via composition, was never actually a "build" problem), eval tooling (closed; eval content never will be — it is policy, not code), gateway mechanics (closed for orgs without pre-existing integration debt, e.g. Intility; still open for orgs with it, e.g. Uber/Thomson Reuters, and will likely stay open because the debt is non-transferable), observability — cost/usage metrics (closed via OTel GenAI conventions) vs. engine-internal metrics (open, and being actively kept open by vLLM's own maintainers on cardinality grounds, not by immaturity), quota/chargeback data (closed), quota/chargeback enforcement (open, confirmed no roadmap pressure, and likely to stay open — it's a monetization choice, not an engineering gap), and gateway *governance* (unsettled and actively re-fragmenting even where capability is shared).

- **Position:** The open-source-sufficient/must-build line is set jointly by (1) how recently the capability existed at all — which explains fast convergence at the engine, control-plane and KV-cache layers, where gaps close in roughly 4-18 months once multiple independently-funded efforts chase them — and (2) whether the residual custom work is actually a generic capability or an encoding of this-organization's-own infrastructure or policy (which no open-source project can ever supply, by definition, regardless of maturity); a fourth factor — open-core monetization gates and governance-capture fights inside the nominally-neutral OSS layer itself — produces a class of build-or-fork decisions that track the OSS supply side's incentives rather than either side's technical needs, confirmed to have zero roadmap pressure behind closing in at least one load-bearing case (LiteLLM tag budgets), and growing rather than shrinking as the control-plane projects consolidate around a small number of corporate sponsors.
- **Confidence:** medium-high for the layer-by-layer verdicts (each now rests on a dated, first-person primary source, and four previously-uncertain live-status questions were independently confirmed rather than left as inference); medium for the overall four-factor predictive model, since it is my synthesis across sources rather than any single source's claim, and the "governance-capture is growing" sub-claim rests on one dense but recent GitHub-issue thread plus the Agent Router rename, not a longitudinal trend.
- **Boundary conditions:** This reading applies to the control-plane/gateway/KV-cache/observability/eval/guardrails/quota layers surveyed here, where I have dated, first-person evidence on both sides, now cross-checked against live issue-tracker status. It does not extend cleanly to pure model-serving performance tuning (e.g., custom attention kernels, speculative decoding tuning) where the evidence in this corpus is thinner and the capability-gap story may dominate more simply. It also assumes Kubernetes as the deployment substrate (per the corpus's own consensus claim) for most of the evidence base; the non-Kubernetes case is now confirmed capability-complete on both llm-d and Dynamo, but I found no first-person operator account of either running in production outside Kubernetes, so "ships the feature" and "an operator relies on it" remain distinct claims there.
- **What would change this position:** If a dated primary source showed an organization abandoning a bespoke gateway/policy layer specifically *because* an OSS project absorbed its organization-specific rules (not just its generic routing mechanics) — e.g., a company retiring its own data-residency enforcement code because a CNCF project added configurable jurisdiction-aware routing as a first-class primitive — that would undercut the policy-locus claim that such work is inherently non-transferable. Conversely, if LiteLLM (or a competitor) open-sourced tag-based budget enforcement in response to competitive pressure, that would undercut the "monetization boundaries don't close" sub-claim and should be treated as a capability-gap case after all, not a permanent one — right now the evidence points the other way (stale-closed, no engagement). If vLLM's maintainers reversed position on issue #51186 and merged native OTel GenAI metrics, that would move engine-internal observability from "open, deliberately" to "closed," which would be the single most consequential reversal available to watch for.
- **Evidence weight:** 3 clean, dated, multi-source-corroborated capability-gap-closure cases (control plane via KServe, control plane via llm-d's own non-K8s mode, KV-cache via three independent implementations); 4 pre-existing-infrastructure cases with named, specific mechanisms (Netflix/Triton, Atlassian/TensorRT-LLM, Snowflake/network-perimeter, Thomson Reuters/data-residency); 3 policy-locus cases (T-Tech eval content, LiteLLM budget-rule enforcement, Atlassian model-garden governance); 1 open-core-monetization case confirmed via a dead feature request with an explicit maintainer-silence pattern (LiteLLM tag budgets); 1 governance-capture case with primary GitHub-issue evidence and named objecting/forking parties (GAIE/EPP/llm-d/Dynamo); 1 case of a project actively declining to close a gap on technical-cost grounds rather than simply not having gotten to it yet (vLLM OTel metrics RFC). No case in the corpus contradicts the four-factor framing outright; the main soft spot is that the framing itself is my synthesis, not any one source's explicit claim.

## Open questions

- Whether any organization has run llm-d's or Dynamo's control plane outside Kubernetes in production — both projects now ship the capability, but I found no first-person operator account of using it, so adoption lags shipping by an unknown margin here.
- Whether vLLM's maintainers will reverse their Sep 2026 position on issue #51186 and merge native OTel GenAI engine metrics, or whether the cardinality objection holds and operators are left permanently reading native `vllm:*` Prometheus metrics directly rather than a standardized surface.
- Whether any gateway project (Envoy AI Gateway / Agent Router, Kong, Portkey) closes the dollar-based (not token-based) spend-governance gap that Envoy AI Gateway's own v1.0 GA explicitly left on its roadmap — this is the cleanest remaining "will it close like Moonshot's gap did, or stay open like LiteLLM's" test case to watch.
- Whether the Istio-maintainer objection to the EPP/BBR move into llm-d's repo gets resolved with a genuinely neutral conformance path, or whether NVIDIA Dynamo's and Microsoft KAITO's stalled-PR divergence hardens into a permanent multi-implementation fork — this bears directly on whether "adopt the gateway standard" will mean one thing or three in another year.
