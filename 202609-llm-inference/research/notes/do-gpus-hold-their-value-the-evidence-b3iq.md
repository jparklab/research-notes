### They last.
  * Old cards still get long contracts.
> “CoreWeave signed a contract for Nvidia A100 GPUs that runs into 2029, nine years after the Ampere SKU debuted.”
Mike Intrator, CoreWeave CEO, on the Q2 earnings call · 
  * Six-year-old silicon is still busy.
> “Thanks to CUDA, the A100 GPUs we shipped six years ago are still running at full utilization today, powered by a vastly improved software stack.”
Colette Kress, NVIDIA CFO · 
  * Hardware off contract is rebooked at once.
> “A batch of H100s coming off an expired contract was immediately rebooked at 95% of the original price.”
Mike Intrator, CoreWeave CEO, to CNBC · 
  * Software, not silicon, sets the retirement date.
> “CUDA's compatibility and our massive installed base extend the life in our systems well beyond their original estimated useful life.”
Colette Kress, NVIDIA CFO · 


### They don’t.
  * The long schedules may be accounting, not engineering.
> “Understating depreciation by extending useful life of assets artificially boosts earnings.”
Michael Burry, who put the effect at $176 billion across hyperscalers between 2026 and 2028 · 
  * One hyperscaler shortened its schedule.
> “An increased pace of technology development, particularly in the area of artificial intelligence and machine learning.”
Amazon, in a February filing · 
  * The rental market flipped to oversupply.
> “Don’t buy H100s. The market has flipped from shortage ($8/hr) to oversupplied ($2/hr) because of reserved compute resales, open model finetuning, and decline in new foundation model co’s.”
Eugene Cheah, CEO of featherless.ai · 
  * NVIDIA ships a new architecture roughly every year, faster than any of these schedules.
