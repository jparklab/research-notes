# Interim report: under-documented-layers-audited-absence

**Locus question:** Does ANY named organization publicly document its embedding/reranker serving, its currency-denominated per-team chargeback, its eval gating, or a real regulated-industry inference architecture — or is the absence itself the finding?
**Flavor:** convergent (verification work)

## What the corpus already said

Before this pass, the corpus held each layer only as project documentation or vendor/consulting material, per `coverage-gaps.md` and `redundancy-audit.md`: TEI and Infinity as repo docs with no named adopter; the chargeback mechanism (LiteLLM virtual keys + `x-team-id`/`x-project-id` headers joined to vLLM's Prometheus counters) established only by a Spheron vendor guide and a SysArt consulting piece, neither naming a client; promptfoo and lm-evaluation-harness as repo docs, with T-Tech and Thomson Reuters on record building eval infra in-house instead of adopting either; and the regulated-industry cluster reduced by the redundancy audit to "3 independent voices, of which only 1 is neither vendor nor composite" (the Mycroft Project's fictionalized bank-executive scenario, Prediction Guard's vendor guide naming Noblis via a customer quote, and the Thomson Reuters SovereignAI report, which is media/legal-sector sovereignty, not financial-services regulation).

I re-verified this by direct `note show` and `search --include-body` against the corpus caveat (the suffixed tag undercounts), reading the full bodies of Netflix [[in-house-llm-serving-at-netflix-by-ai-platforms-model-runtime-team-and-by-netfli]], Thomson Reuters [[source-analysis-thomson-continual-learning-of-frontier-models-for-sovereignai]], Atlassian [[atlassians-ai-gateway-best-in-class-model-garden-inside-atlassian]], Uber [[navigating-the-llm-landscape-ubers-innovation-with-genai-gateway]] and [[from-predictive-to-generative-how-michelangelo-accelerates-ubers-ai-journey]], Intility [[how-to-build-an-inference-platform]], T-Tech [[from-production-traffic-to-post-training-building-a-self-hosted-llm]], TEI [[github-huggingfacetext-embeddings-inference-a-blazing-fast-inference-solution-fo]] and [[text-embeddings-inference-hugging-face]], Infinity [[github-michaelfeilinfinity-infinity-is-a-high-throughput-low-latency-serving-eng]], promptfoo [[github-promptfoopromptfoo-test-your-prompts-agents-and-rags-red-teamingpentestin]], lm-evaluation-harness [[github-eleutherailm-evaluation-harness-a-framework-for-few-shot-evaluation-of-la]], the Ogenki reference architecture [[self-hosted-llm-stack-a-solid-foundation-for-an-open-weight-platform-built-to-ev]], the chargeback vendor/consulting trio [[llm-cost-attribution-and-team-budget-tracking]] (TrueFoundry), [[token-budget-management-and-cost-attribution-for-on-premises-llm-inference]] (SysArt), [[gpu-cloud-finops-for-ai-teams-cost-allocation-per-project-chargeback-and-tag-bas]] (Spheron), LiteLLM's own docs [[multi-tenant-architecture-with-litellm-litellm]], and the regulated-industry pair [[the-llm-is-extraordinary-the-bank-cant-use-it]] and [[best-self-hosted-ai-models-for-regulated-industries-financial-services-and-gover]]. I also pulled the vLLM maintainer design thread [[vllms-v1-engine-architecture-issue-8779-vllm-projectvllm]], which the corpus had tagged for an unrelated reason but which turns out to be directly load-bearing for the embedding layer.

## What the new sources say

I spent my full 2-source budget on a search-first fetcher mission targeting the two highest-value gaps (a second named embedding-serving adopter, and any genuine regulated-institution first-person architecture). The fetcher ran 5+ distinct search angles for embedding-serving adopters and found none — every hit was vendor/consulting content (Baseten, Spheron, GIGAGPU, Introl, etc.) rather than an operator's own account. It also ran targeted searches against eight named banks/fintechs (Capital One, Nubank, DBS, JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Wells Fargo, HSBC) plus healthcare/insurance, and found zero qualifying first-person serving-architecture accounts — only PR-style product announcements (JPMorgan's "LLM Suite," Goldman's "GS AI Assistant") or model-research posts that don't touch serving infrastructure (Nubank's nuFormer transaction model). It did, however, surface two genuine first-person accounts from **government** bodies, which I fetched:

**[[using-self-hosted-large-language-models-llms-securely-in-government-digital-trad]]** — UK Department for Business and Trade (DBT), official gov.uk blog, July 9 2025 (within the run's recency window), by a named DDaT data scientist. DBT self-hosts LLMs (Llama, Mistral) on AWS SageMaker inside an internal, internet-isolated VPC ("Data Workspace") for trade-agreement topic modelling, long-document extraction, and parliamentary-sentiment analysis, after a 15-user pilot. On cost: "One of the biggest challenges in deploying LLMs is cost... by deploying LLMs within Data Workspace, we're able to share infrastructure across multiple teams and projects." This is shared-infrastructure language, not currency chargeback — no invoice, no per-team dollar figure, no billing mechanism described.

**[[productionising-large-language-models-in-government-by-ng-shangru-ai-practice-an]]** — GovTech Singapore, Whole-of-Government NLP team, Medium (Nov 2023, by named engineer Ng Shangru). This is the single most directly relevant source fetched for Layer 1. Quote: "We moved towards using thenlper's gte-large which outperformed text-ada-embedding-002. This model was also open-source and allowed for on-premise deployments... The embedding model was hosted with Gunicorn running FastAPI workers that uses the sentence-transformers library." They explicitly frame this as a milestone: "Being able to host the embedding ourselves marked a significant milestone in being fully capable of supporting all LLM / RAG applications within GCC, and even on-premise if necessary." Their generation stack at the time was HuggingFace TGI serving Llama-2-70b-chat-hf on an on-premise A100 server for Secret-classified workloads, reached via a documented progression from BLOOM-176B on Ray+Alpa (47% GPU utilization, ~$30K/month) to GCP A100 + `transformers-bloom-inference`. **Dated caveat:** this is a Nov 2023 account — squarely in the "TGI-as-default, pre-vLLM" era the run directives flag as stale. TEI's rerank endpoint didn't ship until TEI v0.4.0 and Infinity was immature at this point, so GovTech's bespoke Gunicorn/FastAPI/sentence-transformers wrapper may simply reflect what existed at the time, not a considered rejection of TEI/Infinity.

## Evidence synthesis

### Layer 1 — Embedding/reranker serving

TEI and Infinity remain exactly what the width sweep found: well-documented, well-licensed (TEI Apache-2.0 since Apr 2024, rerank endpoint since v0.4.0; Infinity MIT) OSS projects with **zero named production adopters** — not in their own docs, not via exhaustive web search across multiple query angles. Infinity's most recent tagged release is Aug 2025 (14 months stale as of this Oct 2026 fetch), a second, independent signal of waning momentum beyond pure absence-of-adopters.

But the layer is not a total blank. Two named organizations do disclose how they serve embeddings, and **neither uses TEI or Infinity**:

- **Netflix** [[in-house-llm-serving-at-netflix-by-ai-platforms-model-runtime-team-and-by-netfli]] states its workload mix "broadened to include embedding generation, prefill-only inference for ranking and retrieval, autoregressive decoding, and custom models" and that this breadth was a *reason* it picked vLLM as its paved-path engine over TensorRT-LLM. Embedding generation is folded into the same vLLM-on-Triton serving platform as everything else — no separate embedding server is mentioned.
- **GovTech Singapore** [[productionising-large-language-models-in-government-by-ng-shangru-ai-practice-an]] built a bespoke embedding server (Gunicorn + FastAPI + sentence-transformers wrapping `gte-large`) rather than adopting a dedicated project — though this predates TEI/Infinity's maturity, so it is weak evidence of a *considered* build-over-adopt choice.

Separately, vLLM's own embedding/reranker support is not original-design: the GitHub maintainer thread [[vllms-v1-engine-architecture-issue-8779-vllm-projectvllm]] shows a community contributor (`noooop`) proposing a "Workflow Defined Engine" refactor specifically to support encode-only/embedding/reranker models (BGE, GTE, Arctic Embed families), submitting a ~10,000-line draft PR that maintainer `DarkLight1337` said could not be merged at once and had to be staged. So vLLM's embedding capability is a retrofitted, community-driven addition to a generation-first engine, not a first-class design goal — which makes it notable that the one named org that uses it for embeddings (Netflix) treats it as unremarkable enough not to discuss further. None of the other named platforms in the corpus (Atlassian, Thomson Reuters, Intility, T-Tech) mention embedding serving at all in their own posts — a silence worth naming, since T-Tech and Thomson Reuters are both RAG-adjacent-enough operations that embeddings are plausibly in scope and simply went undiscussed.

### Layer 2 — Currency-denominated per-team chargeback

This absence is the cleanest of the four and held up completely under additional searching. Every named-org account that touches cost stops at *visibility*, never *settlement*:

- Uber's GenAI Gateway: "generation of audit logs for comprehensive cost attribution" [[navigating-the-llm-landscape-ubers-innovation-with-genai-gateway]] — a logging capability, no currency, no team-level bill.
- Atlassian's AI Gateway: "robust rate limiting, quota management, and cost tracking... to manage spend across dozens of teams" [[atlassians-ai-gateway-best-in-class-model-garden-inside-atlassian]] — cost *tracking*, explicitly paired with rate limiting and quota, not chargeback.
- Intility ships token usage "to be persisted for billing" [[how-to-build-an-inference-platform]] — the single closest named-org statement to actual billing language in the corpus, but ambiguous: Intility is a managed-IT/cloud provider whose "usage service" plausibly bills *external* customers for a resold platform, not internal teams, and no dollar figures or invoice mechanics are given.
- DBT UK: "share infrastructure across multiple teams and projects, optimising our costs" [[using-self-hosted-large-language-models-llms-securely-in-government-digital-trad]] — cost-sharing, not chargeback.
- T-Tech reports a platform-level 2.8–3.9x per-token cost reduction versus third-party inference [[from-production-traffic-to-post-training-building-a-self-hosted-llm]] and Thomson Reuters reports a 19.7% saving on one model [[source-analysis-thomson-continual-learning-of-frontier-models-for-sovereignai]] — both are aggregate platform economics, not team-level billing.

The full mechanism — LiteLLM virtual keys injecting `x-team-id`/`x-project-id`, joined against vLLM's `/metrics` token counters, rolled up into a showback/chargeback report — exists only in Spheron's vendor guide and a SysArt consulting piece, neither naming a client, plus LiteLLM's own product docs describing the capability as something the proxy *can* do (and gating cross-team cost attribution behind its Enterprise tier). A dedicated follow-up search for a named org describing actually billing internal teams in currency (excluding LiteLLM/TrueFoundry/Spheron/SysArt, already known) returned nothing beyond the same vendor/consulting content.

### Layer 3 — Evaluation gating

Also confirmed absent for the specific claim at issue — a named org adopting an off-the-shelf harness as a **deployment gate**. The asymmetry the locus rationale predicted is sharp and goes the opposite direction from a naive "OSS gets adopted" prior:

- **T-Tech** [[from-production-traffic-to-post-training-building-a-self-hosted-llm]]: custom production-traffic error taxonomy, in-house stratified benchmarks scored by deterministic verifiers or calibrated LLM judges, per-axis GRPO experts merged via SLERP — all bespoke, while the serving engine itself (vLLM) is off-the-shelf.
- **Thomson Reuters** [[source-analysis-thomson-continual-learning-of-frontier-models-for-sovereignai]]: built CapTrack + Scales++ specifically because no existing benchmark gave "a multifaceted view of model quality beyond aggregate accuracy."
- **promptfoo** and **lm-evaluation-harness** both carry only self-reported adopter claims inside their own READMEs — promptfoo's "powers LLM apps serving 10M+ users in production" and "used by OpenAI and Anthropic" is the project's own marketing line, unverified by an independent account; lm-eval-harness's "used internally by dozens of organizations including NVIDIA, Cohere, BigScience, BigCode, Nous Research, and Mosaic ML" is likewise the README's own claim, and even taken at face value describes offline benchmark *scoring* (its design target — no CI/CD framing anywhere in its README), not pre-deployment gating.
- The one place an off-the-shelf harness is actually wired into a pipeline is the Ogenki reference architecture [[self-hosted-llm-stack-a-solid-foundation-for-an-open-weight-platform-built-to-ev]], which runs Promptfoo as a **nightly CronJob** for continuous quality monitoring — but its own fetcher flagged it as "a personal/demo project, not a named company's internal production service," and a nightly monitoring job is a materially weaker claim than a PR-blocking gate.

So the two most detailed first-person engineering accounts in the entire corpus — the ones with the most to say about *everything else* in their stacks — both independently concluded existing eval tooling didn't fit and built their own. That is itself the finding, not a gap in search effort.

### Layer 4 — Regulated-industry architecture

This is where new fetching changed the shape of the finding rather than just confirming it. The hypothesis going in was that regulated industry does not publish internal architectures at all. That is **false as a blanket claim** but **true for commercial regulated industry specifically**:

- Exhaustive targeted search across eight named banks/fintechs (Capital One, Nubank, DBS, JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Wells Fargo, HSBC) plus healthcare payers/insurers found zero first-person serving-architecture accounts — only product-announcement PR (JPMorgan "LLM Suite," Goldman "GS AI Assistant," Morgan Stanley's OpenAI partnership) or model-research posts that stop at the model layer without describing serving infrastructure (Nubank's nuFormer transaction model). The two financial-services sources already in the corpus remain a fictionalized composite scenario [[the-llm-is-extraordinary-the-bank-cant-use-it]] and a vendor guide citing a customer (Noblis, a federal contractor, not a bank) via a secondhand CTO quote [[best-self-hosted-ai-models-for-regulated-industries-financial-services-and-gover]].
- Two **government** bodies, by contrast, published detailed first-person accounts: UK DBT [[using-self-hosted-large-language-models-llms-securely-in-government-digital-trad]] (self-hosted SageMaker inside an isolated VPC, July 2025) and GovTech Singapore [[productionising-large-language-models-in-government-by-ng-shangru-ai-practice-an]] (on-premise TGI serving Secret-classified workloads, Nov 2023 — stale, but unusually detailed, including the custom embedding server described above).

Government bodies operate under data-sensitivity constraints at least as strict as commercial finance (DBT handles pre-decision trade-policy negotiations; GovTech Singapore explicitly serves Secret-classified workloads), yet they publish architecture detail that no named bank, insurer, or healthcare payer in this corpus does. The likely driver is not confidentiality constraint but **disclosure incentive**: government technology blogs exist for civic-transparency and recruitment purposes and face no competitive-disclosure cost, whereas a bank's infrastructure choices are commercially sensitive in a way a trade department's are not, and banking regulators (OCC, Fed, SR 11-7 model-risk guidance cited in the Mycroft piece) create compliance *review* obligations internally without creating any *publication* obligation externally.

## Committed position

Across all four layers, the pattern is not "nobody writes about this" but "nobody who writes about this uses the dedicated off-the-shelf project built for it, and the organizations most willing to publish operational detail skew toward the layers where publishing makes them look good." Generation-serving-engine choice (vLLM vs. TensorRT-LLM vs. Triton) is a competitive-flex story with cost and latency numbers worth bragging about, and it dominates the corpus's named-adopter evidence; embedding serving, currency chargeback, and eval-gate adoption are back-office cost-accounting and quality-assurance decisions that read as mundane even when disclosed, and the few disclosures that exist (T-Tech's and Thomson Reuters' eval infra, Netflix's and GovTech's embedding handling) describe bespoke builds, not adoption of TEI, Infinity, promptfoo, or lm-evaluation-harness — which inverts the naive expectation that popular, well-licensed OSS projects attract documented adopters. The regulated-industry absence is real for commercial finance/insurance/healthcare specifically, but it is not a generic "regulated entities don't publish" phenomenon: two government bodies under comparable data-sensitivity constraints did publish, which means the correct explanation is disclosure incentive (civic/recruitment blogging vs. competitive-sensitivity silence), not confidentiality requirement per se — a distinction worth making explicit in the final report rather than collapsing "regulated" into one undifferentiated silent category.

- **Position:** (1) Embedding/reranker serving: TEI and Infinity have zero documented named production adopters despite exhaustive search; the only two named orgs that disclose embedding serving (Netflix, GovTech Singapore) built it themselves — one folded into vLLM's generation engine, one as a bespoke FastAPI/sentence-transformers wrapper. (2) Currency chargeback: confirmed absent — every named-org account tops out at cost *visibility/tracking* language, never a described currency transfer between teams; the full mechanism is vendor/consulting-only. (3) Eval gating: confirmed absent for deployment-gate adoption — the two most detailed first-person accounts in the corpus (T-Tech, Thomson Reuters) both built bespoke eval infra instead of adopting promptfoo/lm-eval-harness, whose only "adopter" evidence is self-reported README claims. (4) Regulated-industry architecture: the absence is real and intrinsic for *commercial* finance/insurance/healthcare specifically, but is falsified for *government* bodies — two (UK DBT, GovTech Singapore) published genuine first-person architecture under comparable data-sensitivity constraints, so the final report should narrow "regulated industry" to "regulated commercial industry" when stating this absence.
- **Confidence:** high for layers 2 and 3 (absence held after dedicated adversarial searching across multiple query angles and 8+ named institutions, with no near-misses); medium for layer 1 (two positive data points exist, but one is stale/2023 and neither is a TEI/Infinity adopter, so the "no dedicated-project adopter" claim is solid while the broader "nobody serves embeddings" framing would be wrong and must not appear in the final report); medium-high for layer 4 (the government/commercial split is well-evidenced by two strong sources but rests on only 2 positive and ~10 negative data points, so a single leaked bank engineering post could complicate, not just confirm, the pattern).
- **Boundary conditions:** These findings apply to *publicly disclosed, named, first-person* accounts as of Oct 2026. They say nothing about what is actually run internally — absence of publication is not evidence of absence of practice (LiteLLM's enterprise tier existing specifically for cross-team cost attribution implies someone is buying it; promptfoo's self-reported adopter list may be accurate even though unverified). The eval-gating finding is scoped to "harness as pre-deployment CI/CD gate" — if the bar were lowered to "any automated quality check in a pipeline," Ogenki's nightly CronJob and the AI-guardrails literature would count, but those are a different, already-well-covered layer (guardrails/observability), not eval-as-gate.
- **What would change this position:** A single named bank/insurer/healthcare-payer engineering blog, conference talk, or regulatory filing describing its own LLM serving architecture would overturn the Layer 4 commercial-finance absence specifically (government counter-examples already exist, so the bar for Layer 4 is a *commercial* account). A named org's blog post stating "we charge Team X $Y for Z tokens this month" would overturn Layer 2. A named org's blog post saying "we gate releases on an lm-evaluation-harness/promptfoo score threshold before serving" would overturn Layer 3. A named org's post describing production TEI/Infinity deployment (not just repository stars or self-reported README claims) would overturn Layer 1's "zero dedicated-project adopters" claim, though it would not change the broader "embedding serving is the least-disclosed generation-adjacent layer" framing given how much digging it took to find even two tangential examples.
- **Evidence weight:** Layer 1 — 2 named positive accounts (Netflix, GovTech, neither using the dedicated projects) vs. 0 TEI/Infinity adopters found across repo docs + 5 search angles. Layer 2 — 0 named currency-chargeback accounts found vs. 3 vendor/consulting sources establishing the mechanism in the abstract, plus 1 dedicated adversarial search confirming no new leads. Layer 3 — 2 named orgs confirming in-house build (T-Tech, Thomson Reuters) vs. 2 OSS projects with only self-reported, non-independent adopter claims. Layer 4 — 2 named government accounts (positive) vs. ~10 named commercial institutions searched with zero qualifying results, plus 1 composite scenario and 1 vendor-with-customer-quote already in the corpus.

## Open questions

- Did GovTech Singapore's team ever reconsider TEI or Infinity once those projects matured (their post is from Nov 2023, TEI's rerank endpoint shipped in a later v0.4.0, and the post itself names forward-looking exploration of vLLM/Triton)? No later GovTech post was found in this budget; a follow-up search specifically for a GovTech or Singapore GovTech 2025/2026 update could resolve whether the custom embedding wrapper persisted or was replaced.
- Is Intility's "persisted for billing" language about internal team chargeback or external customer billing (Intility resells this platform to clients)? The source text is ambiguous and a direct question to Intility's engineering team (unreachable within this pipeline) would resolve it; absent that, treat the Layer 2 absence as holding.
- Does any bank/insurer disclose architecture only in a regulatory filing (10-K risk-factor language, SR 11-7 model-validation documentation, or a central-bank AI-adoption survey response) rather than an engineering blog? This search mode (financial filings / regulator surveys rather than engineering blogs) was not attempted within the 2-source budget and is the most promising unexplored lead for falsifying the Layer 4 commercial absence.
